# Ingredient Extraction Reference

Flavor compound profiles for ingredients used in ice cream. Each entry lists primary aroma/flavor compounds, their solubility in water, alcohol, and fat, temperature sensitivities, and flavor/smell notes.

Entries are in **alphabetical order** by ingredient. When adding a new entry, insert it in the correct alphabetical position.

**Lookup:** scan for `## [Ingredient]` (case-insensitive). If a matching heading is found, use those values — do not run a web search.

**Solubility scale:** Insoluble → Slightly soluble → Moderately soluble → Soluble → Miscible

**LogP guide:** LogP > 2 = strongly fat-soluble | LogP 0–2 = moderate | LogP < 0 = prefers water

## Black Sesame Seeds (Sesamum indicum — roasted, black variety)

**GC-MS composition (roasted seeds):** Character defined by Maillard reaction products formed during toasting. 2-Methoxy-4-vinylphenol (4-vinylguaiacol) carries the highest OAV (160) and dominates the burnt-clove-smoky impression. Furaneol (OAV 80) provides caramel sweetness. 2,5-Dimethylpyrazine and related alkylpyrazines deliver roasted/cocoa/nutty notes. Sesamol forms from sesamolin degradation above ~180°C and provides the characteristic earthy-phenolic sesame base note. **Black vs. white:** Black seeds contain statistically lower levels of 2,5-dimethylpyrazine and ethyl acetate than white sesame; the overall aroma profile is similar but black sesame reads as earthier and more bitter.

| Furaneol (DMHF) | caramel, strawberry-candy, fruity-jammy, sweet | 176 g/L at 20°C | Soluble | LogP 0.95 | BP ~275°C; stable below 130°C; formed from d-fructose via Maillard reaction during roasting |

## Basil / Sweet Basil (Ocimum basilicum)

**Chemotype note:** Sweet/European/Italian basil — the standard culinary type. Composition: Linalool 60–80%, Methyl chavicol (estragole) 10–25%, 1,8-Cineole 6–12%. Thai, clove, and other chemotypes differ significantly (eugenol- or methyl eugenol-dominant) and require separate profiling. **Acid caution:** linalool isomerizes in acidic conditions — add any acid only after straining and cooling.

## Cinnamon (Cinnamomum verum — Ceylon bark; or C. cassia/aromaticum — Cassia bark)

**GC-MS composition (bark essential oil):**
- **Ceylon (C. verum):** (E)-Cinnamaldehyde 65–80%, Eugenol 4–5%, β-Caryophyllene 3–4%, Linalool 2–3%, (E)-Cinnamyl acetate 2–3%. Rounder, more complex profile.
- **Cassia (C. cassia / C. aromaticum):** (E)-Cinnamaldehyde 85–93%, eugenol trace. Simpler, more intensely "cinnamon," less nuance.
- **Coumarin warning:** Cassia contains 5–16 mg coumarin per tsp (3g); EFSA TDI is 0.1 mg/kg/day. One tsp Cassia can approach or exceed this limit. Ceylon contains only trace coumarin (<1 mg/kg). Use Ceylon in any recipe where cinnamon is a primary or high-dose flavor.

## Green Peas (Pisum sativum — frozen garden peas)

**GC-MS composition:** Character defined by methoxypyrazines at ultra-low thresholds — IBMP (3-isobutyl-2-methoxypyrazine) and IPMP (3-isopropyl-2-methoxypyrazine) together produce the classic green pea note at ng/L concentrations. Hexanal (high OAV from lipoxygenase activity on membrane lipids) and 1-octen-3-ol provide the fresh-green and earthy-fungal backdrop. Nonanal adds a waxy, fatty-floral note. **Processing note:** Blanching deactivates lipoxygenase, preventing further hexanal generation and fixing the flavor profile; sharper volatile green aldehydes (trans-2-hexenal) are essentially lost above 80°C.

## Jasmine Tea (Jasminum sambac — green tea scented with jasmine flowers)

**GC-MS composition:** Linalool is the dominant odorant (highest OAV in brewed tea); benzyl acetate and methyl anthranilate provide the sweet fruity-floral character of fresh jasmine; indole contributes the characteristic jasmine note at trace concentrations (fecal above threshold — dose-sensitive). Short-chain aldehydes (2-methylpropanal etc., BP 64–93°C) are essentially fully lost in warm brewing. **Brew at 75–80°C for 2–3 min; strain immediately; cool to <20°C without re-heating.** Shares linalool with lychee, making them complementary partners.

## Lychee (Litchi chinensis — fresh or puréed fruit)

**GC-MS composition:** cis-Rose oxide is the defining character compound (highest OAV; only the (−)-cis isomer is odor-active at ~0.5 ppb threshold). Geraniol and linalool provide the rose-floral backbone; furaneol adds sweet caramel-fruity depth; methional (trace, heat-labile) contributes a cooked-savory background note found in ripe and processed fruit. The profile is strongly floral-fruity; rose oxide + geraniol + linalool together produce the recognizable lychee-Gewürztraminer aroma.

Oil divides into ~88% monoterpenes and ~12% phenylpropanoids. Myristicin is the character-defining phenylpropanoid. **Volatile oils begin escaping within minutes of grinding — use freshly grated nutmeg.**

## Oolong Tea (Camellia sinensis — partially oxidized leaf)

**Key odorants by odor activity value (OAV):** Nerolidol (OAV 108–184), Indole (OAV 96–138), Linalool (OAV 63–87), β-Damascenone (OAV 29–59), Geraniol (floral backbone). Short-chain aldehydes (2-methylpropanal, 3-methylbutanal) have very high OAVs but boiling points of 64–93°C — they volatilize in any warm infusion. The persistent floral-fruity character is defined by nerolidol, linalool, damascenone, and indole.

**Critical extraction note — steep leaves directly in dairy, not in water first.** Nerolidol (LogP 5.68) and β-Damascenone (LogP 3.4–4.0) are strongly fat-soluble and extract poorly into water. Brewing in water and adding the liquid to custard leaves the most distinctive compounds behind in the spent leaves.

## Wasabi Root (Wasabia japonica / Eutrema japonicum)

**Key compounds:** Allyl isothiocyanate (AITC) — primary pungent volatile; 3-Butenyl isothiocyanate (3-BITC) — secondary pungent; 6-(Methylsulfinyl)hexyl isothiocyanate (6-MSITC) — sweet-vegetable complexity unique to real wasabi. All three are enzymatically generated (not pre-formed): myrosinase enzyme, released on cell damage, converts glucosinolate precursors (principally sinigrin) to isothiocyanates. **Heat above ~60°C permanently deactivates myrosinase** — root must be grated and rested 3–5 minutes at room temperature before any heat contact. If root is heated before grating, no isothiocyanates form at all.
