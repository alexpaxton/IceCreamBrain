# Wasabi Tincture (Sweet-Forward, Low-Heat)

## Notes on Flavor

**On "sweet and floral":** The sweet note is real — that's 6-MSITC, the compound unique to genuine wasabi. But wasabi root doesn't have documented floral compounds (no terpene alcohols, no esters). If you're sensing something floral, it may be the clean, non-acrid edge of 6-MSITC by contrast with AITC's harshness, or a cross-compound impression.

**On neutral grain spirit:** Ethanol prevents the aqueous hydrolysis that turns AITC into garlic-sulfur off-notes. NGS is the right solvent.

## The Key Tension

AITC and 6-MSITC both dissolve readily in ethanol — you can't easily separate them on extraction. But AITC is volatile (vapor pressure 3.7 mmHg at 20°C) and 6-MSITC is essentially non-volatile (high MW, polar sulfoxide). You can exploit this through venting.

## Method

1. Grate fresh wasabi root fine. Rest 3–5 minutes uncovered at room temperature — enzymatic rest. Skip this and you get no AITC or 6-MSITC from fresh root.
2. Add grated paste to neutral grain spirit (roughly 1:5–1:8 by weight; adjust to taste). Do not seal tightly — loosely cover with cloth or a jar ring without the seal.
3. Infuse at room temperature for 30–60 minutes. AITC extracts fast; 6-MSITC extracts more slowly but is stable.
4. Strain, then let the strained tincture sit open or loosely covered for 1–2 hours at room temperature. AITC off-gasses preferentially; 6-MSITC stays behind.
5. Seal and refrigerate. Use within a week — AITC continues to decline, 6-MSITC holds.

The aged/vented tincture will be noticeably sweeter and less sharp than a freshly-strained one.

## Delivery Method

**Atomized on top:** AITC dominates the nose because it's the volatile fraction. You'll get the nasal hit even if the tincture is 6-MSITC-dominant, because atomization releases the volatile phase first. Works against the sweet goal.

**Dropped in before shaking (recommended):** AITC disperses into the drink and dilutes; the sweeter 6-MSITC note stays more present on the palate rather than the nose. Brief contact with ice water during shaking causes minimal hydrolysis at cocktail time scales.
