# Blue Ribbon Chocolate (Allulose Split) v1

Dana Cree's Blue Ribbon Chocolate rebuilt for a 5°F / −15°C serving temperature at 1.5 gallons,
and pushed deliberately below the original's sweetness. The glucose syrup is gone and the whole
sweetener load is split 60/40 sucrose to allulose. Allulose carries 1.9x the freezing power of
sucrose per gram at 0.7x the sweetness, so raising its share lets total added sweetener fall while
PAC stays pinned at 30: 720g of sweetener here against the 1000g the original proportions would
call for at this scale, or 14.4% of the base against 20%. The chocolate offsets part of it —
533g of 70% dark and 266g of milk chocolate carry 245g of sucrose in on their own — but net
sweetness still lands about 18% below the original. That is the point. Texture agent is a 50/50
guar/xanthan pair at 0.16% of the base; the two are synergistic, so 8g of the blend does more than
8g of either alone, which matters with a sweetener that softens as hard as allulose does. Salt is
2g per 1000g. The allulose goes in off heat: it browns well below sucrose's caramelization point
and will scorch against a hot pan, and any Maillard color it develops muddies the chocolate.

## Ingredients

| Qty | Ingredient |
|-----|-----------|
| 2398g | Whole milk |
| 852g | Heavy cream (36% fat) |
| 107g | Nonfat dry milk powder |
| 533g | Dark chocolate, 70%, chopped |
| 266g | Milk chocolate, chopped |
| 107g | Cocoa powder, unsweetened |
| 432g | Sucrose |
| 288g | Allulose |
| 4g | Guar gum |
| 4g | Xanthan gum |
| 10g | Salt |

> Yield 5001g base. FPD index of the added sweetener 1.36 — in range. Full-recipe PAC 30.01,
> predicting −15.00°C / 5.0°F. Freezing point −4.17°C / 24.5°F. TS 47.5%, fat 14.14%, MSNF 7.93%,
> water frozen at serving temp 81.6%. At 25% overrun this yields about 5.7 L / 1.5 gallons.

## Tools

- Stainless steel pots
- Stainless steel mixing bowls
- Instant-read thermometer
- Balloon whisk
- Immersion blender
- Metal mesh strainers
- Milligram precision scale
- Food scale
- Whynter ice cream maker

## Procedure

**Split the batch before you start.** The Whynter holds 2.1 qt including overrun, so it takes
roughly 1250g of base per churn. This recipe is four churns. Make and cure the full 4998g as one
batch, then weigh it into four portions at churn time.

**Melt the chocolates.** Bring 2 inches of water to a simmer in a pot. Nest a metal bowl over it
without touching the water and melt the 533g dark chocolate, 266g milk chocolate, 107g cocoa
powder, and 10g salt together, stirring until smooth. Wipe the bottom of the bowl dry and hold it
somewhere warm. It must still be fluid when the hot dairy arrives.

**Dry-blend the powders.** Whisk the 4g guar, 4g xanthan, and 107g nonfat dry milk into 250g of
the sucrose, taken from the 432g. Reserve the remaining 182g sucrose and keep the 288g allulose
separate. Gums clump on contact with water, and the sugar crystals are what keep the particles
apart until they disperse.

**Boil the dairy.** Combine 2398g whole milk and 852g heavy cream in a heavy-bottomed pot over
medium-high heat and bring to a full rolling boil, whisking often. Milk powder and cocoa both
scorch, so keep the whisk moving against the bottom.

**Cook in the powders.** Whisk in the gum-milk-powder-sugar blend, drop to a low simmer, and cook
2 minutes, whisking constantly. This is the guar's hydration window — it needs the heat, xanthan
does not.

**Combine with the chocolate.** Whisk one-third of the hot base into the melted chocolate, then
the rest in two more additions, whisking between each. Adding hot base to chocolate gradually
rather than the reverse prevents unmelted flecks, which read as grit once frozen.

**Sweeten off heat.** Stir in the reserved 182g sucrose and the 288g allulose. Whisk until fully
dissolved. Do not put the allulose anywhere near direct heat.

**Emulsify.** Immersion blend the warm base 45 seconds. Cocoa particles settle and the gums have
not fully set their network yet; this is the moment to disperse both.

**Chill and strain.** Cool over an ice bath to 10°C / 50°F or below, then pass through a fine mesh
strainer.

**Cure.** Refrigerate below 4°C / 39°F for at least 4 hours, overnight preferred. Guar keeps
hydrating cold and the chocolate fat needs the time to crystallize.

**Churn in four batches.** Weigh out ~1250g per run. Keep the remaining base refrigerated between
churns and re-whisk each portion before it goes in.

**Harden.** Press plastic wrap on the surface, seal, and hold at −15°C / 5°F for at least 8 hours.

## Watch in Testing

- **81.6% water frozen at 5°F is just above the 75–80% band.** It may scoop a touch firm despite
  landing on PAC 30. Total solids at 47.5% is high — that is the chocolate — and high TS reads
  dense and chewy. If it eats too stiff, the lever is trading sucrose for more allulose at constant
  total sweetener mass, not adding sweetener.
- **Sweetness is ~18% below the original**, and ~7% below the 70/30 split. This is the intended
  direction, but it is far enough down that the cocoa's bitterness has less to push against. Taste
  the cured base cold before churning — flavors read weaker at 4°C / 39°F than at room temperature
  but the ranking holds. If it reads bitter rather than clean, the next lever is the chocolate
  ratio, not the sweetener: shift 60g from the dark to the milk chocolate and re-run PAC.
- **Allulose is at 5.8% of the base.** Above roughly 6–7% it can read cooling or faintly metallic
  to some palates. This sits just under that, but note it if you taste anything odd on the finish
  before blaming the chocolate.
- **Allulose browns at low temperature.** Any scorching in the pot shows up as a burnt note against
  the cocoa. If the base tastes even slightly roasted where it should not, the allulose touched
  heat.
- **Fat is 13.95%**, most of it cocoa butter rather than butterfat. Cocoa butter sets much harder
  than butterfat, which pushes the same direction as the high TS. Watch for a waxy coating on the
  palate.
- **Combined gum load is 0.16%.** If it reads slimy or stringy, cut the guar to 2.5g before
  touching the xanthan.
- **Salt at 2g/1000g** is a third less than the source recipe's 3g/1000g. With sweetness already
  down, the salt is doing more of the work of making the chocolate read as chocolate. If it tastes
  muted rather than under-sweet, try 15g total on the next version.
