# Bone Marrow Cognac Ice Cream v1

A Philadelphia-style base built around two ingredients that pull the formula in opposite
directions. Roasted marrow brings fat with no water and no milk solids; the cognac reduction
brings water and a little residual ethanol with no solids at all. Cream is cut to 340g to make
room for the marrow's fat, milk to 300g for the reduction's water, and 45g of nonfat dry milk
goes back in to replace the MSNF lost from both cuts. Simmering the roasted marrow directly in
the base and straining afterward extracts the Maillard products from roasting, not just the fat
— rendered fat alone carries almost none of that. The sugar is split four ways and tuned to a
5°F / −15°C serving temperature: allulose for freezing power at low sweetness cost, dark
muscovado for molasses depth that meets the reduction's oak and the marrow's savoriness, sucrose
for baseline sweetness, glucose powder for body without added water. Reducing cognac drives off
the bright fruit esters first, so what concentrates is the heavy end — vanillin, guaiacol,
gamma-nonanolactone, the ionones. Oak, vanilla, and rancio, which is what marrow wants. Marrow
fat sets near 40°C / 104°F, well above butterfat, so sunflower lecithin and a guar/xanthan pair
do the emulsion work egg yolk would do in a custard.

## Ingredients

| Qty | Ingredient |
|-----|-----------|
| 340g | Heavy cream |
| 300g | Whole milk |
| 45g | Nonfat dry milk powder |
| 65g | Roasted bone marrow, scooped *(simmered and strained out; delivers ~50g fat)* |
| 70g | Sucrose |
| 46g | Allulose |
| 21g | Glucose powder DE 42 |
| 15g | Dark muscovado sugar |
| 3g | Sunflower lecithin |
| 1g | Xanthan gum |
| 1g | Guar gum |
| 2.5g | Salt |
| 80g | Cognac reduction *(from 320g cognac)* |

> FPD index: 1.32 — in range. Full-recipe PAC 30.02, predicting a −15.01°C / 5.0°F serving
> temperature. Freezing point −4.08°C / 24.6°F, TS 43.5%, yield ~975g. PAC is insensitive to
> marrow yield: ±8g of delivered fat moves serving temp by 0.13°C.

## Tools

- Stainless steel saucier pan
- Stainless steel pots
- Aluminum baking sheet
- Metal mesh strainers
- Immersion blender
- Instant-read thermometer
- Milligram precision scale
- Food scale
- Whynter ice cream maker

## Procedure

**Roast the bones.** Roast marrow bones on a sheet at 220°C / 425°F for 15–20 minutes, until the marrow is soft through and browned at the exposed ends. Scoop out the marrow and weigh 65g. Some rendered fat will have pooled on the sheet — scrape it in, it counts toward the 65g and carries roast flavor.

**Reduce the cognac.** Reduce 320g cognac to 80g in a wide saucier over low heat at a bare simmer, not a rolling boil. A hard boil strips the mid-volatile esters you want to keep along with the ethanol. Target 4:1 by weight; weigh the pan rather than eyeballing volume. Cool completely and reserve.

**Dry-blend the powders.** Whisk the xanthan, guar, lecithin, nonfat dry milk, and glucose powder into the sucrose and allulose before any liquid touches them. They clump instantly in water otherwise. Keep the muscovado separate — it's damp and will seize the blend.

**Simmer the marrow into the base.** Combine the cream and milk, whisk in the sugar-powder blend, then the muscovado and salt. Add the 65g of scooped marrow and bring to 80–85°C / 176–185°F. Hold covered for 20 minutes, stirring occasionally to break the marrow apart as it softens. Do not boil — a hard boil toughens the marrow protein into rubbery threads and drives off the roast aromatics you just built. Keep it covered to limit evaporation.

**Strain.** Pour through a fine mesh strainer while hot so the fat stays molten and passes through. Press the solids lightly to release clinging fat, but don't force them through. Discard the solids. You should have roughly **895g** of strained base; if you're short, top up with water to 895g before continuing, since evaporation during the simmer concentrates the sugars and raises the freezing point.

**Emulsify.** With the strained base still above 50°C / 122°F, run an immersion blender hard for a full 60 seconds. The base must stay above marrow fat's 40°C / 104°F set point through this step or the fat seizes into specks. Do this only after straining — blending beforehand shreds the marrow solids and clogs the mesh.

**Add the reduction cold.** Chill below 4°C / 39°F, then stir in the 80g cognac reduction. Do not add it to a warm base — those aromatics already survived one boil and reheating loses them a second time.

**Age and churn.** Rest at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, so the guar fully hydrates. Churn in the Whynter, then harden at 5°F / −15°C for at least 6 hours before scooping.
