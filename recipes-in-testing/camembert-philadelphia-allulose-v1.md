# Camembert Philadelphia (Allulose) v1

A no-egg base that uses camembert as both the main flavor and a main source of fat and protein. 300g
of camembert paste gives 73g fat and 59g protein, so heavy cream drops to 230g and the recipe stays
near 16.5% fat. Protein is about 7.5%, which is about 3.3x a standard Philadelphia base, so expect a
dense texture that is a bit chewy, like cheesecake. The rind is steeped in the base as it heats, to
pull mushroom and earthy notes from the bloomy surface, and then strained out before the cure, so
ammonia notes do not continue to build overnight. The cheese brings about 6.4g of salt, and 1.5g more
is added, for about 0.79% salt in total. That salt adds about 4.6 points of PAC. Sweeteners are 40%
allulose and 60% sucrose by weight, 180g in total, balanced for serving at 5°F / -15°C (PAC 31.3,
approximate freezing point -4.61°C / 23.7°F, 81.2% water frozen). Xanthan and guar are used in equal
parts.

## Ingredients

| Qty | Ingredient |
|-----|-----------|
| 286.5g | Whole milk |
| 230g | Heavy cream (36% fat) |
| 300g | Camembert paste, rind removed (from 2 × 250g wheels) |
| all | Camembert rind from both wheels (~90g), for steeping only |
| 108g | Sucrose |
| 72g | Allulose |
| 1.5g | Salt |
| 1g | Xanthan gum |
| 1g | Guar gum |

## Tools

- Paring knife
- Vitamix 5500 blender
- Stainless steel saucier pan
- Instant-read thermometer
- Metal mesh strainer (fine)
- Silicone spatulas
- Immersion blender
- Milligram precision scale
- Food scale
- Whynter ice cream maker

## Procedure

**Separate the cheese.** Use cheese that has been out of the fridge for about 30 minutes. Cut off the rind with the paring knife, but leave a thin layer of paste on it. Weigh 300g of paste. Put the rind aside. You will have about 110g of paste left over.

**Disperse the gums.** Whisk 1g xanthan and 1g guar into the 108g sucrose until there are no clumps.

**Blend.** Put the milk, cream, camembert paste, gum-sucrose mix, allulose, and salt in the Vitamix. Blend on low, then go up to medium for 30–45 seconds until smooth. Do not blend on high, or the base gets foamy.

**Steep the rind while heating.** Pour the base into the saucier and add the rind pieces. Heat over medium-low and stir all the time with a spatula, scraping the bottom, because cheese protein sticks and scorches. Bring to 75°C / 167°F and hold for 2 minutes so the guar hydrates. Total contact time between rind and base is about 10–15 minutes.

**Strain the rind.** Strain through the fine mesh strainer and press the rind with the spatula to get the liquid out. The rind will hold back about 10–20g of base. Throw the rind away.

**Re-emulsify.** Run the immersion blender in the strained base for 15–20 seconds. This breaks up any fat or protein that separated from the cheese during heating.

**Cure.** Chill below 4°C / 39°F and rest for at least 8 hours.
