# Cereal Bowl & Lemongrass Philadelphia v1

An eggless base whose entire flavor comes from a multi-day cold steep of corn flakes, Fruity Pebbles,
and lemongrass, all strained out before churning. The three are steeped in separate dairy phases
because they want different solvents and different clocks: lemongrass citral is fat-loving (logP ~3.4)
and goes into the cream, while the corn flake Maillard compounds and the Fruity Pebbles flavor coating
are water-soluble and go into the milk. Citral and the artificial citrus note in the Pebbles share
compounds, so the lemongrass sharpens the cereal rather than sitting beside it; the corn flakes are the
contrast, a toasted grain floor with no compound overlap. Sweetener is 40% allulose / 60% sucrose by
weight, which carries 1.36x the freezing power of straight sucrose per gram, so 1139g of blend reaches
PAC 29.7 and lands the serving temperature right on 5°F without an unreasonable sugar load. No sugar,
salt, or gum enters the steep — they go in after straining, so the final balance is exact no matter how
much dairy the cereal eats.

## Procedure

**Pasteurize the dairy (skip if both are ultra-pasteurized).** The cereal sits in this base for three days, so start it sterile. Heat 4400g of the whole milk to 74°C / 165°F, hold 30 seconds, and chill hard to 4°C / 39°F or below. Do the same with all 2850g cream. Reserve the remaining 400g milk cold and untouched — it is the gum carrier and never meets cereal.

**Day 1 — start the corn flakes.** Stir 535g corn flakes into the 4400g chilled milk. Hold at 4°C / 39°F or below for 72 hours. The flakes will saturate in the first hour and collapse into a slurry by day two; that is expected. Cold water does not gelatinize corn starch, so the slurry stays granular rather than turning to paste.

**Day 3 — add the Fruity Pebbles and start the lemongrass.** Stir 300g Fruity Pebbles into the corn flake milk 24 hours before straining. Their flavor is a surface coating and comes off in minutes, so a longer soak buys nothing and only bleeds more dye. At the same time, bruise 270g lemongrass hard along its length with the rolling pin, cut into 2-inch pieces, and submerge in the 2850g chilled cream. Hold both at 4°C / 39°F or below for 24 hours.

**Check the pH before straining.** Fresh milk reads about 6.7. If the cereal milk has dropped below 6.4 it has soured and the steep is done for — the acid will also cyclize the lemongrass citral into p-cymene and p-cresol, which read medicinal. Discard and shorten the next run to 48 hours.

**Strain the cream.** Pour the lemongrass cream through a mesh strainer and press the stalks with a spatula. Weigh the recovery — expect 2550-2600g. Top up with fresh cold cream to exactly **2536g**, or hold back the excess if you got more.

**Strain the cereal milk.** Work it through the tamis in batches. Let each batch drain by gravity for 20 minutes, then press with the spatula. Press only — do not wring or force. Hard pressure drives fine starch through the 60-mesh and the base goes chalky. Weigh the recovery and top up with fresh cold milk to exactly **2431g**.

**Hydrate the gums.** Whisk 6.7g xanthan and 6.7g guar into 60g of the sugar, taken from the 683g. Whisk that into the reserved 400g cold milk, heat to 80°C / 176°F, hold 2 minutes for the guar, then chill to 4°C / 39°F. Guar only reaches full viscosity with heat, and nothing after this point gets heated.

**Combine and sweeten.** Combine the 2536g strained cream, 2431g strained cereal milk, and the 400g chilled gum milk. Immersion blend in 168g nonfat dry milk, 456g allulose, the remaining 623g sugar, and 13.4g salt until fully dissolved. Blend in short bursts and stop before it foams.

**Cure.** Refrigerate below 4°C / 39°F for at least 8 hours.

**Churn.** About 6.2L of base. Run roughly 1200mL per batch in the Whynter, 5 batches, so there is headroom for overrun.

**Harden.** At least 12 hours at the coldest freezer setting, then hold at -15°C / 5°F to serve.

## Watch in Testing

- **Dairy recovery is the largest unknown in this recipe.** The 2850g cream and 4400g milk assume the solids hold about 1.0g per gram for lemongrass and 2.2g per gram for cereal. If the tamis gives up less than that, you will come up short and need fresh dairy to top up — buy an extra quart of each before starting. Every downstream number depends on hitting 2536g and 2431g exactly.
- **The 2-gallon yield rests on a 25% overrun assumption.** At 20% this makes 1.96 gal; at 30%, 2.12 gal. Measure the overrun on the first batch and scale the next run instead of guessing again.
- **Combined gum load is 0.338% of the water phase**, just over the 0.30% ceiling. This is what a 1g/kg xanthan floor plus a 50/50 guar split produces. If it eats slick, stringy, or slow-melting, cut both to 5.4g and re-churn before touching anything else.
- **Fruity Pebbles carries Red 40, Yellow 6, Blue 1, and Blue 2.** Mixed together in a cream base they do not read as rainbow — they read as grey-lavender. The 24-hour window limits it, but if v1 comes out muddy, drop the Pebbles to a 4-hour steep or leave the blue-dyed pieces out.
- **81.9% water frozen at 5°F** is above the 75-80% band, so this scoops firm straight from a -15°C / 5°F holding freezer. Temper 10 minutes rather than adding sugar; past PAC 30 more sweetener buys almost no ice fraction.
- **Sweetness lands at 16.8% sucrose-equivalent**, above the 14-16% norm, because the cereal leaches its own sugar in. Correct for a cereal-milk flavor, but if it reads cloying, cut the sucrose to 620g and accept PAC 28.7.
- **Starch fines will pass the tamis** no matter how gently you press. A slight opacity and extra body in the strained milk is normal; a chalky or floury finish means you pressed too hard.
- **Citral is acid-sensitive.** Do not add any acidic ingredient to this base at any point, and do not let the steep run long enough to sour. The pH check before straining is not optional.
