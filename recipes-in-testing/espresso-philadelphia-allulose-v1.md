# Espresso Philadelphia (Allulose) v1

A no-egg, low-carbohydrate espresso base carrying its entire sweetener load as allulose and its
body from whey protein isolate rather than milk powder. Allulose has 1.9x the freezing power of
sucrose per gram but only 0.7x the sweetness, so 138g reaches PAC 30 while tasting about as sweet
as 97g of sugar. Whey isolate replaces skim milk powder to supply protein without dragging lactose
in behind it, which drops digestible carbohydrate to 2g per 100g. Refined coconut oil carries fat
to 18% for the same reason. All coffee flavor comes from fresh espresso stirred in cold at the last
moment, which protects 2-furfurylthiol, the compound that actually reads as "espresso" and which
oxidizes and cooks off fast. The whey goes in after the base has cooled, because it denatures above
65°C / 149°F and aggregates badly near the pH espresso brings.

## Tools

- Balloon whisk
- Breville espresso machine

- Food scale
- Whynter ice cream maker

## Procedure

**Disperse the gums.** Whisk 1g xanthan gum and 0.7g guar gum into 20g of the allulose, taken from the 138g. There is no milk powder in this version, so the allulose crystals are the carrier that keeps the gum particles apart. Reserve the remaining 118g.

**Hydrate.** Whisk the gum-allulose blend into 400g cold cream and 173g cold whole milk. Immersion blend 20 seconds, stopping before it foams. Heat to 75°C / 167°F and hold 2 minutes for the guar.

**Emulsify the oil.** Holding at 75°C / 167°F, stream in 20.7g melted refined coconut oil while running the immersion blender. Blend 45 seconds until opaque with no oil visible at the surface. The casein in the cream and milk is doing the emulsifying here, which is why the oil goes in before the whey.

**Cool, then add the whey.** Cool below 40°C / 104°F before whisking in 32g whey protein isolate. Use the balloon whisk by hand, not the immersion blender — whey isolate foams hard under shear and the air will not come back out. Above 65°C / 149°F the whey denatures and turns gritty, so do not shortcut this cooling step.

**Sweeten.** Below 20°C / 68°F, stir in the reserved 118g allulose and 2g salt until dissolved.

**Chill.** Refrigerate below 4°C / 39°F for at least 4 hours.

**Pull and chill the espresso.** Pull 210g as close to churn time as possible, closer to a 1:1.5 ratio than 1:2 for more aromatic load per gram of water. Roughly 6 to 8 double shots. Chill fast over an ice bath.

**Combine cold and churn.** Stir the cold espresso into the cold base gently. Cold is doing real work here: native whey at espresso's pH is stable cold but aggregates when warm. Churn immediately.

**Harden.** At least 8 hours at the coldest freezer setting, then hold at -15°C / 5°F to serve.

## Watch in Testing

- **Coconut oil is ~90% saturated** and stays harder than butterfat at -15°C / 5°F. Firmness and a slight coating mouthfeel are the main texture risks. If too hard, the lever is fat composition, not more allulose — past PAC 30 the % water frozen barely moves.
- **Whey builds different body than milk powder.** Skim milk powder is ~80% casein, which binds water and gives chewy body; whey isolate has none. Expect a cleaner, less chewy body and possibly higher overrun. If it eats thin, adjust the gums, not the whey.
- **Protein at 4.69%** is above the ~3.8% of a standard base. Deliberate, but whey turns chalky as it climbs. If chalky, drop the isolate to 24g.
- **Emulsifier pool is thin.** About 13.6g of casein from the cream and milk holds 20.7g of coconut oil. If it churns greasy or weeps, add 1.5g sunflower lecithin — far under the 7.8%-of-fat ceiling.
- **Combined gum load is 0.17%.** Xanthan and guar are synergistic. If stringy or slick, cut guar to 0.4g before touching the xanthan.
- **210g of espresso is a lot of acid** into an eggless base with little sugar mass buffering it. If it reads thin or sharp rather than round, pull shorter ristretto at lower volume rather than adding allulose.
