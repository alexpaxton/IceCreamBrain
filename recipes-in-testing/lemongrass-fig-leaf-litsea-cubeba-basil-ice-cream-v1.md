# Lemongrass, Fig Leaf, Litsea Cubeba & Basil Ice Cream

**Style:** Philadelphia Style
**Makes:** between 1 and 1½ quarts

Four contrasting infusions with a complementary citral core. Lemongrass and litsea cubeba reinforce each other's sharp lemony-citral character; fig leaf adds deep rose-jammy-green depth; basil brings sweet floral and cooling eucalyptus notes. Each infusion is handled separately to honor its ideal extraction conditions — fig leaf cold-sealed overnight to protect its volatile green top note, the citral pair and basil run simultaneously in the sous vide at the same temperature. The vacuum sealer eliminates aromatic vapor loss that would otherwise plague open warm infusions of these ingredients.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | g | % |
|------------|---|---|
| Heavy cream | 380g | 38% |
| Whole milk | 400g | 40% |
| Granulated sugar | 150g | 15% |
| Glucose syrup | 50g | 5% |
| Whole milk powder | 20g | 2% |
| **Total base** | **1000g** | **100%** |

*Infusion ingredients (prepared in stages, strained out before Step 6):*
- Fresh fig leaves — 6 large leaves (~12g), torn
- Lemongrass stalks — 2 stalks (~50g), bruised and cut into 2-inch pieces
- Litsea cubeba dried berries — 5g, lightly crushed
- Fresh basil leaves (sweet/Italian) — 20g

Xanthan gum — 1g | ¼ tsp, blended into the fully chilled base before churning

Target ranges (Philadelphia Style): water 55–65%, fat 12–20%, sugars 18–28%, protein 2–4%

## Method

**Step 1 — Fig leaf cold infusion (night before).** Combine the torn fig leaves with 100g of the cream in a chamber vacuum sealer bag. Seal under full vacuum and refrigerate for 18–24 hours, weighted flat if possible to keep leaves submerged.

**Step 2 — Set up sous vide.** The following day, preheat the immersion circulator bath to 62°C.

**Step 3 — Lemongrass and litsea cubeba infusion.** Combine the lemongrass pieces and litsea cubeba berries with 160g of the cream in a chamber vacuum sealer bag. Seal under full vacuum and submerge in the 62°C bath for 45 minutes.

**Step 4 — Basil infusion.** Combine the basil leaves with the remaining 120g of cream in a separate chamber vacuum sealer bag. Seal under full vacuum and submerge in the same 62°C bath for 25 minutes. Steps 3 and 4 run simultaneously.

**Step 5 — Strain all infused creams.** Remove bags from the bath. Strain each bag through a fine metal mesh strainer, pressing gently on the solids with a silicone spatula to extract cream. Discard solids. Strain the cold fig leaf bag as well. Combine all three strained infused creams — you should have approximately 360–375g.

**Step 6 — Combine milk powder and sugar.** Mix the milk powder and sugar together in a small bowl.

**Step 7 — Boil the dairy.** Combine the infused cream, milk, and glucose in the stainless steel saucier pan over medium-high heat. Cook, whisking occasionally, until it reaches a full rolling boil.

**Step 8 — Add milk powder mixture.** Whisk the milk powder and sugar into the saucier. Reduce heat to a low simmer and cook for 2 minutes, whisking to prevent scorching.

**Step 9 — Chill.** Pour the base into a shallow metal bowl set into an ice bath. Stir occasionally with a silicone spatula until cool to the touch (50°F / 10°C or below), checking with the instant-read thermometer.

**Step 10 — Add xanthan gum.** Transfer the chilled base to the Vitamix with the vacuum pitcher attachment. Add 1g xanthan gum, seal the lid, pull vacuum, and blend for 20 seconds. This prevents air incorporation into the base. Return to the bowl.

**Step 11 — Cure.** Transfer to the refrigerator and cure for at least 4 hours, preferably overnight.

**Step 12 — Churn.** Churn in the Whynter until it holds soft-serve texture, 20–30 minutes.

**Step 13 — Harden.** Transfer to an airtight container, press parchment paper directly to the surface, and freeze 4–12 hours until hard-packed.

## Notes

- **Acid timing:** Do not add any acidic ingredient (citrus juice, citric acid) until the base is fully strained and chilled. Citral from both lemongrass and litsea cubeba cyclizes rapidly under acidic conditions and produces p-cymene off-flavors.
- **Fig leaf green note:** The cold overnight infusion preserves trans-2-hexenal, the sharp fresh-cut note that defines fig leaf. If you skip it and do a warm sous vide infusion instead (62°C, 20 min), the base will read as rounder and more rose-jammy with most of the green quality gone.
- **Litsea cubeba sourcing:** Dried berries are available at specialty spice shops and some Asian grocery stores. If unavailable, substitute 1 drop of food-grade litsea cubeba essential oil, added to the fully chilled base after Step 9 — never into hot liquid.
- **Basil variation:** Sweet/Italian basil is the baseline. Thai basil shifts the character toward anise-licorice and pairs especially well with the fig leaf's rose-honey note; purple basil is more floral and slightly less green.
- **Adjusting citral intensity:** As written, the fig leaf and basil are the more assertive notes against the cream. To push the lemongrass/litsea citral core forward, extend Bag 2 to 60 minutes or add a third stalk of lemongrass.
- **Cream yield loss:** Expect ~5–10% cream loss to the strained solids across the three bags. This slightly reduces the base yield but does not meaningfully affect macros.
