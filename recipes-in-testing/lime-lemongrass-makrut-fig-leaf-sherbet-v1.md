# Lime Lemongrass Makrut Fig Leaf Sherbet v1

Dairy lime sherbet built on Dana Cree's Blank Slate, with no buttermilk and no added acid. Fresh lime juice at 10% supplies all the tartness; the aroma comes from four steeped botanicals split across two sous vide bags so each runs at its own temperature. Every aroma compound here is fat-soluble (limonene and γ-terpinene in lime zest, citronellal in makrut leaf, geranial and neral in lemongrass, β-damascenone and the sesquiterpenes in fig leaf), so all steeping happens in the dairy before any acid goes in. Acid would cyclize citral and citronellal into off-flavors. Makrut and zest go low and short at 63°C / 145°F, where citronellal stays bright. Lemongrass and toasted fig leaf go hot at 80°C / 176°F: lemongrass is fibrous and its citral is heat-stable at neutral pH, and 80°C fully inactivates ficin, the milk-clotting protease in fig leaf sap, as a backup to the toast. The mix is balanced for serving at -15°C / 5°F rather than the repo default of -12°C / 10.4°F, which is why sweetener sits at 31.5% of the batch. Sweetener is 40/60 allulose/sucrose. Nonfat milk powder lifts protein from 1.8% to 2.5%.

> Citric acid in the lime juice (~6%) is modeled as dextrose-equivalent. The PAC table has no citric acid entry, so its ~1.2 PAC contribution is an estimate. Botanicals are not counted; they are strained out.

**Sweetness.** About 27.7% sucrose-equivalent, against ~19.5% in the Blank Slate. This is the cost of balancing at 5°F. The lime acid offsets part of it.

**Stabilizer dosing basis.** Gums are dosed on the water phase (557g).

| | Rate | Working range | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xanthan + guar | 0.25% of water | 0.15–0.30% | 1.40g |
| Xanthan : guar | 1:1 | 1:1 to 1:2 | 0.70g each |

Final mix pH will be about 4.3–4.6. Guar loses some viscosity below pH 4.6; xanthan is unaffected by acid. Both are hydrated before the juice goes in.

**Botanical doses are first guesses.** Steep time is fixed; adjust strength in v2 by leaf weight, not by time. More leaf for a shorter time gives more aroma and less bitterness than less leaf for longer, since the aroma compounds extract quickly and the tannins and chlorophyll extract slowly.

## Procedure

**Fig leaves.** Wear gloves when handling raw leaves; the sap contains psoralen, which burns skin under UV. Cut off the stems and wipe the leaves dry. Toast in the dry frying pan over medium heat, pressing flat and turning, until the whole leaf is crisp and snaps cleanly, center vein included. Any soft or flexible area may still hold active ficin. Target a coconut-vanilla smell and dull olive to light brown color. Black or acrid means burnt; discard and redo.

**Other botanicals.** Peel the zest with the paring knife, taking only the green layer (pith gives bitterness), then mince. Tear the makrut leaves and remove the center vein. Smash the lemongrass with the back of the chef knife, then slice 2–3mm thin.

**Gum premix.** Reserve 15g of the sucrose. Whisk the xanthan and guar into it until there are no streaks. Set aside dry.

**Boil the dairy.** Whisk the milk powder into the remaining sucrose and the allulose before adding to the cold milk and cream, so the powder does not clump. Add the salt. Bring to a boil and simmer 2 minutes.

**Cool and split.** Chill over an ice bath to 10°C / 50°F or below. A warm base boils under vacuum in the chamber sealer and loses volatiles before the seal closes. Weigh the base and divide it in half by weight.

**Bag.** Bag A: half the base, makrut leaves, zest. Bag B: half the base, lemongrass, toasted fig leaves. Seal both and flatten to about 2–3cm / 1in thick.

**Steep bag A.** 63°C / 145°F for 25–30 minutes, counted from when the bag reaches temperature (allow 10–15 minutes come-up). Makrut sets this time; past it, citronellal turns soapy and shifts to minty-medicinal isopulegol. Move the sealed bag to ice water.

**Steep bag B.** Raise the bath to 80°C / 176°F. Steep 30–45 minutes from when the bag reaches temperature. Do not exceed 45 minutes; fig leaf tannins set the ceiling. Move the sealed bag to ice water.

**Strain.** Chill both bags at least 10 minutes before opening, so the volatiles stay in the liquid. Strain both through the fine mesh into one bowl and press the botanicals. Weigh and restore to 898.6g with whole milk.

**Gums.** With the base at or below 10°C / 50°F in the Vitamix on medium, rain in the gum premix and ramp to high for 30 seconds.

**Cure.** Hold at or below 4°C / 39°F at least 4 hours so the guar hydrates before it meets acid.

**Juice.** Squeeze the limes the same day. Just before churning, stream the juice into the cold base with the Vitamix on low so it disperses before casein can form visible curds near pH 4.6. Record the pH.

**Harden and store.** Hold at -15°C / 5°F. At -12°C / 10.4°F the mix is ~75% frozen and will be soft.

## Watch on test

- **Ficin.** Before the juice goes in, leave a small cup of strained base at room temperature for 30 minutes. Grain, thickening, or bitterness means ficin survived both the toast and the 80°C steep.
- **Makrut.** It is the easiest botanical to overdose. Soapy means too many leaves. Minty-medicinal means too hot or too long.
- **Lemongrass.** If it reads faint, raise the weight in v2. The 80°C bag already gives it full extraction.
- **Sweetness.** If too sweet, the levers are juice (more acid) and sweetener. Both move PAC.
- **Curdling.** Check for grain on the tongue after churning. The milk powder adds ~36% more casein than the same base without it.
- **Set.** FPD is -6.6°C / 20.1°F. Judge hardness only at 5°F.
