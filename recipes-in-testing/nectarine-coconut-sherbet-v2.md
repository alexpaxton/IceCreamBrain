# Nectarine Coconut Sherbet v2

Fruit-forward sherbet anchored by a large volume of nectarine puree. Cocojune coconut yogurt provides tang and light body — its live-culture fermented character complements stone fruit well. The light coconut milk fills out the base liquid without adding coconut richness. Allulose replaces glucose to boost PAC without adding sweetness, appropriate for sweet peak-season nectarines. Sunflower lecithin compensates for the low protein in both coconut products.

## Changes from v1

**Observations:** Flavor and texture were good, but nectarine presence was weaker than desired. Tested with +0.5g salt, +2g coconut oil, 10g honey added, and lemon juice omitted.

**Adjustments:**
- Nectarine puree raised to 69% of the batch by weight (963g to 1263.7g) vs 48.7% in v1
- Coconut milk and yogurt cut ~60% from v1 to make room at the same batch weight
- Coconut oil raised to 42.4g to restore fat to the v1 level of 4.19%, offsetting the reduced coconut milk and yogurt
- Guar gum added alongside xanthan at 1:1 to tighten the emulsion around the added free coconut oil; both gums and the lecithin are now dosed off the water and fat phases rather than fixed weights
- Sucrose and allulose retrimmed to hold PAC at v1 levels while the extra fruit sugar comes forward
- Honey from the test not carried forward; its sweetness redistributed into sucrose and allulose
- Lemon juice removed

> PAC: 30.16 — FPD: -2.61°C / 27.3°F — % water frozen at -12°C / 10.4°F: ~80% — Fat: 4.19% — Water: 72.6% — Nectarine: 69.0%

**Stabilizer dosing basis.** Gums are dosed on the water phase (1329.0g), lecithin on the fat phase (76.75g), since gums build viscosity only in water and lecithin works only at the oil-water interface.

| | Rate | Working range | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xanthan + guar | 0.20% of water | 0.15–0.30% | 2.66g |
| Xanthan : guar | 1:1 | 1:1 to 1:2 | 1.33g each |
| Lecithin | 7.8% of fat | 6–10% | 5.99g |

**Watch on test:** 55% of the fat is now free coconut oil rather than fat arriving pre-emulsified in the coconut milk and yogurt, so the emulsion is the weak point. Lecithin is held at 7.8% deliberately: flavor neutrality outranks emulsion tightness here, and a waxy or slightly greasy texture is the accepted trade rather than any beany or bitter note from the lecithin. If the texture needs help, raise the gums toward 0.30% of water first. Do not raise lecithin.

## Tools

- Stainless steel saucier pan
- Instant-read thermometer
- Vitamix 5500 blender
- Milligram precision scale
- Whynter 2.1 Qt. ice cream maker

## Procedure

**Premix the gums.** Reserve 10g of the sucrose. Whisk the xanthan and guar into it until evenly dispersed with no visible streaks. Set aside dry. The sugar keeps the gum particles separated so they hydrate individually instead of clumping.

**Dissolve sugars.** Warm coconut milk in the saucier to 60°C / 140°F. Add coconut oil and whisk until melted. Whisk in the remaining sucrose, the allulose, and the salt until fully dissolved. Cool to room temperature.

**Blend base.** Combine cooled coconut milk, coconut yogurt, and nectarine puree in the Vitamix. Stream in sunflower lecithin with the machine running on medium, then blend 30 seconds at high.

**Add gums.** With the Vitamix running on medium, rain in the reserved sugar-gum premix through the lid opening and immediately ramp to high. Blend 30 seconds. Guar needs more shear and more time than xanthan alone did.

**Chill and churn.** Chill to ≤4°C / 39°F and hold at least 4 hours before churning; guar hydrates slowly in the cold and the xanthan-guar synergy will not develop until it does. Churn per Whynter instructions, then harden at least 2 hours.
