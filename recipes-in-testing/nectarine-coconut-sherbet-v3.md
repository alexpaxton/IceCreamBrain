# Nectarine Coconut Sherbet v3

Fruit-forward sherbet anchored by a large volume of nectarine puree. Nature's Charm evaporated coconut milk carries the coconut character and fills out the base liquid; the concentrated coconut solids give more coconut presence per gram of water than a canned light coconut milk. The coconut oil is infused with fresh nectarine skins first, so the fat phase carries fruit aroma instead of diluting it — the skin lactones and norisoprenoids are strongly fat-soluble and land in the oil rather than the water. Allulose replaces glucose to boost PAC without adding sweetness, appropriate for sweet peak-season nectarines. Sunflower lecithin compensates for the low protein in the coconut products. Acid is set by taste with tartaric acid at the end.

## Changes from v2

**Observations:** Ingredient swap, not a test result. Cocojune coconut yogurt is out of the build, and the Costco/TCC canned coconut milk is replaced by Nature's Charm evaporated coconut milk. Two process changes go in at the same time: the coconut oil is infused with nectarine skins, and acid is dosed to taste at the end.

**Adjustments:**
- Cocojune coconut yogurt removed
- Coconut milk (Costco/TCC) replaced by Nature's Charm evaporated coconut milk at 260.5g, taking over the full 260.5g the two coconut products held in v2. Batch weight unchanged.
- Coconut oil raised 42.5g to 52.8g. The evaporated coconut milk is ~6.7% fat against the ~14% of the canned milk and the yogurt it replaced, so free oil makes up the shortfall and fat holds at 4.19%.
- Coconut oil is now infused with fresh nectarine skins (sub-recipe below). Weight into the base is unchanged at 52.8g, so macros and PAC are unaffected.
- Sucrose cut 88.7g to 73.0g and allulose raised 164.8g to 170.2g. The extra coconut oil takes 10.3g of the batch weight, so the sugar budget shrinks; the sucrose-to-allulose ratio shifts to hold PAC at 30.16 in the smaller budget.
- Tartaric acid added to taste at the end. v2 had no acid at all once the yogurt tang and the v1 lemon juice were both gone.
- Nectarine puree, salt, lecithin, and gum weights unchanged. Fruit stays at 69.0%.

**Stabilizer dosing basis.** Gums are dosed on the water phase (1332.9g), lecithin on the fat phase (76.75g), since gums build viscosity only in water and lecithin works only at the oil-water interface.

| | Rate | Working range | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xanthan + guar | 0.20% of water | 0.15–0.30% | 2.67g |
| Xanthan : guar | 1:1 | 1:1 to 1:2 | 1.33g each |
| Lecithin | 7.8% of fat | 6–10% | 5.99g |

**Acid dosing.** Start around 0.1% of batch weight (1.8g) and work up. Sorbet range is roughly 0.1–0.4% (1.8–7.3g here).

- Add the acid **after** the gums are fully hydrated and the base is chilled, not before. Guar loses viscosity below pH 4 and hydrolyses slowly at that pH; let it build body at the puree's own pH first.
- Dissolve the acid in 50g of the finished chilled base, then blend that back in. Do not add the crystals dry — they will not disperse evenly and you will taste hot spots.
- The evaporated coconut milk has no protein on the label, so there is no curdling risk from the acid.
- **PAC impact:** the PAC table has no tartaric acid entry. Derived from molecular weight (150.09 against sucrose 342.3) and partial first-proton dissociation at this pH, each gram adds roughly **+0.13 to +0.21 PAC**. At 1.8g that is +0.23 to +0.38; at 7.3g it is +0.95 to +1.55. Cancelling it costs roughly 1.2–2.1g of allulose per gram of acid. This is an estimate, not a measured value.

**Watch on test:** 68% of the fat is now free coconut oil, up from 55% in v2, so the emulsion is the weak point and it is weaker than it was. Lecithin is held at 7.8% deliberately: flavor neutrality outranks emulsion tightness here, and a waxy or slightly greasy texture is the accepted trade rather than any beany or bitter note from the lecithin. If the texture needs help, raise the gums toward 0.30% of water first. Do not raise lecithin.

## Tools

- Stainless steel saucier pan
- Instant-read thermometer
- Sous vide circulator
- Chamber vacuum sealer
- Vegetable peeler
- Fine mesh strainer or tamis (60-mesh)
- Vitamix 5500 blender
- Milligram precision scale
- Whynter 2.1 Qt. ice cream maker

## Procedure

**Infuse the oil.** Make the nectarine skin coconut oil (sub-recipe below) before starting the base. Weigh out 52.8g of the finished oil.

**Premix the gums.** Reserve 10g of the sucrose. Whisk the xanthan and guar into it until evenly dispersed with no visible streaks. Set aside dry. The sugar keeps the gum particles separated so they hydrate individually instead of clumping.

**Dissolve sugars.** Warm the evaporated coconut milk in the saucier to 60°C / 140°F. Add the infused coconut oil and whisk until melted. Whisk in the remaining sucrose, the allulose, and the salt until fully dissolved. Cool to room temperature. Do not take this above 60°C / 140°F — the infused aroma is already in the oil and there is nothing left to extract by heating it further.

**Blend base.** Combine the cooled coconut milk mixture and nectarine puree in the Vitamix. Stream in sunflower lecithin with the machine running on medium, then blend 30 seconds at high.

**Add gums.** With the Vitamix running on medium, rain in the reserved sugar-gum premix through the lid opening and immediately ramp to high. Blend 30 seconds. Guar needs more shear and more time than xanthan alone did.

**Chill.** Chill to ≤4°C / 39°F and hold at least 4 hours; guar hydrates slowly in the cold and the xanthan-guar synergy will not develop until it does.

**Set the acid.** Pull 50g of the chilled base into a small bowl. Dissolve the tartaric acid into it, blend it back into the main base, and taste. Repeat in small steps until the acid is where you want it. Taste cold — the base will read flatter frozen than it does liquid, so err slightly sharp.

**Churn.** Churn per Whynter instructions, then harden at least 2 hours.

## Nectarine Skin Coconut Oil

*Makes: ~55g — supplies the 52.8g of coconut oil in the main recipe*

Method A (sous vide warm extraction). The nectarine skin compounds that matter here are fat-soluble and heat-stable: γ-decalactone (LogP 2.7), δ-decalactone (LogP 2.5), γ-nonalactone (LogP 2.2), theaspirane (LogP 3.7), β-ionone (LogP 4.0), and β-damascenone (LogP 3.4–4.0) all boil well above 190°C and survive custard temperatures. The vacuum bag is what makes the method work: hexyl acetate and benzaldehyde start volatilising above 60°C in an open vessel, and a sealed bag holds them in. Benzaldehyde (LogP 1.4) will partly stay behind in the skin water — the puree already carries that marzipan note.

Skins are the right part to use, not flesh. β-ionone and β-damascenone are carotenoid breakdown products and the skin is where the carotenoids concentrate.

### Ingredients

| Qty | Ingredient |
|-----|-----------|
| 17.5g | Fresh nectarine skins |
| 70g | Coconut oil |

### Method

1. **Peel.** Take the skins off ripe nectarines with a vegetable peeler. Aim for skin with as little flesh attached as possible — flesh carries water and sugar into the oil and neither one helps.
2. **Bag.** Melt the coconut oil to liquid. Combine with the skins in a vacuum bag and seal on the chamber sealer.
3. **Extract.** Cook sous vide at 63°C / 145°F for 2 hours. Do not go above 70°C / 158°F.
4. **Strain.** Pass through the tamis or a fine mesh strainer. Do not press hard — pressing squeezes skin juice into the oil.
5. **Separate.** Refrigerate until the coconut oil sets solid, then lift the fat cake off any water that has separated underneath and discard the water. Coconut oil solidifies around 24°C / 75°F, which makes this clean.
6. **Weigh.** Remelt and weigh. If the yield is under 52.8g, top up with plain melted coconut oil. This is what keeps the fat percentage and PAC on target.

**Storage.** Fresh fruit held in oil is a low-acid anaerobic environment and 63°C / 145°F does not kill spores. Use the oil the same day, or refrigerate it and use within three days. Do not hold it at room temperature.
