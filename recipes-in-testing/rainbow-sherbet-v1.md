# Rainbow Sherbet v1

Three non-dairy coconut sherbets marbled into one 1.5 gallon tub. Peach and pineapple both share lactones with coconut (peach carries gamma-decalactone and delta-decalactone, pineapple carries delta-octalactone and gamma-octalactone, coconut carries delta-octalactone, delta-decalactone and gamma-nonalactone), so those two read as continuous with the base. Raspberry has no lactones worth naming and its character sits in raspberry ketone and the ionones, so it is the contrasting stripe rather than a third variation. All three are balanced to the same PAC. This is the point of the recipe: mismatched hardness makes a marble tear into chunks instead of ribboning, so the added sweetener is different in each flavor specifically to bring all three to the same freeze curve.

Fat is 9%, well above sherbet convention and closer to a gelato. That is a deliberate choice, not a drift.

## Shared Design

| Item | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fruit | 50% by volume | Lands at 47.4–48.1% by weight. The purees run 1.035–1.052 g/mL against a mix at 1.091–1.095 g/mL, so weight% sits below volume%. |
| Fat | 9.00% | 67–70% from coconut milk, the rest from free coconut oil. |
| Sweetener split | 40% allulose / 60% sucrose | Blend is 88% as sweet as sucrose per gram and 1.36x the freezing power. |
| Salt | 2g per 1000g of finished mix | 0.2%. |
| PAC target | 31.5, all three | Matched deliberately. |
| Batch size | 1514 mL mix per flavor | Churns to 0.5 gal at 25% overrun. |

**At 9% fat the formula closes with no added water.** Coconut milk rises to about 307g per 1000g, which supplies 58g of the 90g fat target, and free coconut oil covers the remaining 27–30g. Together they exactly fill the space that fruit and sweetener leave. Water lands at 65.4–66.2% and total solids at 33.8–34.6%, both a large improvement over a low-fat version of the same fruit load. Coconut flavor is also no longer faint; coconut milk is now the second largest ingredient by weight.

**Use unrefined coconut oil, not refined.** Free oil is 30–33% of the fat here. Refined oil contributes no aroma, virgin carries the lactones that tie the base to the peach and pineapple.

**Why PAC stays at 31.5.** Against gelato targets (24–28) that number looks high. It is compensating for the fat. Coconut fat is highly saturated with a steep solid-fat curve, so at serving temperature it is fully crystallized and adds real hardness that the same weight of dairy fat would not. The extra freezing-point depression offsets it. Percent water frozen lands at 79.9–80.0%, inside the 75–80% band.

**Serving temperature: temper to about 10°F, do not reformulate for 5°F.** All the numbers in this recipe are quoted at -12°C / 10.4°F. Held at -15°C / 5°F the ice fraction rises to 82.3% on all three, which is past the 75–80% band and will scoop hard. Sugar is close to useless as a fix at that temperature, because -15°C sits on the flat part of the ice curve where most freezable water is already frozen:

| PAC | Sweetener g/kg | Sweetness, sucrose-eq | Water frozen at 5°F | Water frozen at 10.4°F |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 31.5 (this recipe) | 178 | 20.1% | 82.3% | 80.0% |
| 35 | 205 | 22.4% | 82.0% | 79.4% |
| 40 | 243 | 25.6% | 81.3% | 78.4% |

Raising PAC to 40 costs 36% more sweetener and 5.5 points of sweetness to buy back one point of ice. Actually reaching 80% at 5°F needs PAC near 47, about 295g of sweetener per 1000g, 30% sucrose-equivalent sweetness, and a freezing point near -6°C / 21°F, which would not set properly at normal freezer temperatures. Holding sweetness constant instead and pushing the split to about 95% allulose reaches the same dead end and abandons the 40/60 ratio.

Temperature is the strong lever. Either run the holding freezer at -12°C / 10°F, or hold at -15°C / 5°F and pull the tub 10–15 minutes before scooping.

| Serving temp | Water frozen |
|---|---|
| -15°C / 5.0°F | 82.3% |
| -14°C / 6.8°F | 81.6% |
| -13°C / 8.6°F | 80.9% |
| -12°C / 10.4°F | 80.0% |
| -11°C / 12.2°F | 78.9% |
| -10°C / 14.0°F | 77.6% |

The coconut fat is fully crystallized at both 5°F and 10°F, so none of this difference comes from the fat. It is all ice.

**Stabilizer dosing basis.** Gums are dosed on the water phase, lecithin on the fat phase, since gums build viscosity only in water and lecithin works only at the oil-water interface.

| | Rate | Working range | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xanthan + guar | 0.25% of water | 0.15–0.30% | Mid-range. At this fat level the gums are working on the emulsion around the free oil more than on ice control. |
| Xanthan : guar | 1:1 | 1:1 to 1:2 | |
| Lecithin | 7.8% of fat | 6–10% | Held at the cap. Fix texture with gums, never with more lecithin. |

## Procedure

**Peach puree.** Thaw fully. Blend smooth in the Vitamix. Weigh after blending.

**Pineapple puree.** Thaw, blend smooth, then heat in the saucier to 80°C / 176°F and hold 1 minute. Cool over an ice bath. Frozen pineapple is not blanched, so its bromelain is still active. There is little protein for it to attack in this base, but bromelain stays active in the freezer and will slowly thin the mix across a 1.5 gallon tub's life. The trade is some loss of the fresh esters. Weigh after cooling.

**Raspberry puree.** Thaw fully. Blend, then push through the tamis. Discard the seeds. Weigh the strained puree.

**Per flavor, premix the gums.** Reserve 15g of the sucrose. Whisk the xanthan and guar into it until there are no visible streaks. Set aside dry. The sugar keeps the gum particles separated so they hydrate individually instead of clumping.

**Per flavor, dissolve sugars.** Warm the coconut milk in the saucier to 60°C / 140°F. Add the coconut oil and whisk until melted. Whisk in the remaining sucrose, all the allulose, and the salt until fully dissolved. Cool to room temperature.

**Per flavor, blend.** Combine the cooled coconut mixture and the fruit puree in the Vitamix. Stream in the lecithin with the machine on medium, then blend 30 seconds on high. Do not skip the high-speed stage. There is 45–49g of free oil per batch to drive into the water phase and the lecithin needs shear to place itself at the interface.

**Per flavor, add gums.** With the Vitamix on medium, rain in the reserved sugar-gum premix through the lid opening and immediately ramp to high. Blend 30 seconds. Guar needs more shear and more time than xanthan alone.

**Chill.** Hold all three mixes at or below 4°C / 39°F for at least 4 hours. Guar hydrates slowly in the cold and the xanthan-guar synergy will not develop until it does. The coconut fat will also set during this hold, which is wanted; churning a mix with liquid fat gives a greasier result.

**Churn.** Two runs per flavor, six runs total. Load about 750mL per run to leave headroom for expansion. The compressor allows back-to-back runs with no bowl re-freezing. Scrape each finished run into a flat covered pan and hold in the freezer.

**Marble.** Temper all six pans to about -8°C / 18°F, soft enough to scoop but not runny. Layer scoops into the 1.5 gallon tub, rotating flavors. Draw a spatula through twice, no more. Raspberry bleeds fast and a third pass turns the whole tub pink. Harden at least 6 hours.

## Watch on test

- Hardness and waxiness are the main risk at this fat level, not iciness. Coconut fat is fully solid at -12°C / 10.4°F and 9% of it will read firmer and slicker on the tongue than 9% dairy fat. If it comes out waxy, the first move is dropping fat toward 6%, not adjusting sugars. Serve closer to -10°C / 14°F if it is only slightly firm.
- Judge hardness only after tempering to the serving temperature in the table above. Scooping straight from a 5°F freezer will read as too hard even if the balance is right, and the wrong conclusion there leads to over-sugaring v2.
- Free oil is 30–33% of the fat, well under the 55% in nectarine-coconut-sherbet-v2. The emulsion should be more stable than that recipe, but a greasy or separated look after hardening still points at the blend step. Raise gums toward 0.30% of water first. Do not raise lecithin.
- Coconut is now assertive rather than faint. If it overpowers the peach, cutting coconut oil and raising coconut milk keeps the same fat with a softer coconut note, since the aroma sits in the milk and the free oil mostly contributes fat.
- Raspberry should be the smallest visual share in the tub. It is the most saturated color and the most assertive flavor, and at equal share it will dominate both.
