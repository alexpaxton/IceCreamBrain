# Spring Pea & Pea Shoot Ice Cream

**Style:** Philadelphia Style
**Makes:** between 1 and 1½ quarts

Philadelphia style is exactly right here — no egg yolks means nothing competing with the vivid green. The strategy is to cook the dairy base first, then cold-infuse the pea shoots into the chilled base (preserving all the volatile compounds, especially 2-isobutyl-3-methoxypyrazine, the character-defining "green pea" note), and add the pea puree cold so it never sees heat and its color stays vivid.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | g | % |
|------------|---|---|
| Heavy cream | 350g | 35% |
| Whole milk | 260g | 26% |
| Frozen green peas (for puree) | 200g | 20% |
| Granulated sugar | 120g | 12% |
| Glucose syrup | 50g | 5% |
| Whole milk powder | 20g | 2% |
| **Total base** | **~1000g** | **100%** |

Pea shoots — 60g | about 2 oz, for cold infusion, strained out
Xanthan gum — 1g | ¼ tsp, blended into the fully chilled base before churning

## Method

**1. Make the dairy base.** Mix the milk powder and sugar in a small bowl. Combine cream, milk, and glucose in a stainless steel saucepan over medium-high heat. Whisk occasionally until it reaches a full rolling boil. Add the milk powder mixture, reduce to a low simmer, and cook 2 minutes, whisking constantly. Pour into a shallow bowl set into an ice bath; stir until cooled to 50°F or below. Transfer to the refrigerator and chill thoroughly — at least 4 hours, or overnight.

**2. Blend and cold-infuse the pea shoots.** Bring a pot of water to a boil and prepare an ice bath. Blanch the pea shoots for 15–20 seconds, transfer immediately to the ice bath, drain, and gently squeeze out excess water. Combine the chilled dairy base and blanched pea shoots in the Vitamix and blend on high for 60 seconds. Transfer to a vacuum bag, seal under full vacuum using the chamber vacuum sealer, and refrigerate 12–24 hours. *(No vacuum sealer: seal in a zip-top bag with all air pressed out.)*

**3. Make the pea puree.** Bring a fresh pot of water to a boil and prepare a clean ice bath. Blanch the frozen peas 60–90 seconds until just tender, then transfer immediately to the ice bath. Drain well. Blend in the Vitamix on high until completely smooth, 2–3 minutes. Pass through the 60-mesh tamis, pressing firmly with a silicone spatula to push through all smooth puree and leave fibrous skins behind. Keep refrigerated until needed.

**4. Strain and combine.** Pour the cold-infused base through the 60-mesh tamis, pressing the pea shoot solids gently to recover all liquid. Discard the solids. Add the cold pea puree to the strained base and blend in the Vitamix on high for 30 seconds until fully combined and uniformly vivid green.

**5. Add xanthan.** With the Vitamix running on low, add the xanthan gum and blend for 15 seconds until fully incorporated.

**6. Churn.** Pour the base into the Whynter and churn until thick and soft-serve in consistency, 20–30 minutes.

**7. Harden.** Transfer to an airtight container, press plastic wrap directly onto the surface, cover, and freeze 4–12 hours before serving.

## Notes

- Use frozen peas rather than fresh — they're blanched at harvest and hold greener color and sweeter flavor than fresh peas that have been sitting.
- The pea shoot infusion happens cold, post-cooking, because IBMP (the character compound responsible for the "green pea" smell) is thermally stable but the sharper volatile top notes (hexanal, trans-2-hexenal) are lost above ~50°C. Cold infusion captures the full spectrum.
- Never heat the pea puree — add it only after the base is fully chilled. This is the single biggest lever for a vivid green result.
- Pea starch in the final base is ~0.6% — well below the 1.5% enzyme-treatment threshold. No treatment needed.
- If the green fades before churning (oxidation), a brief squeeze of citric acid solution added after chilling can help stabilize color without affecting flavor.
