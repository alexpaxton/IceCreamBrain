# Spring Pea & Pea Shoot Ice Cream v2

**Style:** Philadelphia Style
**Makes:** between 1 and 1½ quarts

Philadelphia style is exactly right here — no egg yolks means nothing competing with the vivid green. In v2 the extraction method shifts: sugar snap peas and pea shoots are blanched together, blended into avocado oil, and strained through a tamis, producing a vivid green puree that carries both fat-soluble (chlorophyll) and water-soluble (pyrazines) flavor compounds without risking butter. The full base rests overnight before churning, integrating flavors and letting the volatile aromatics bloom in the fat.

## Changes from v1

**Observations:** Blending blanched pea shoots into heavy cream produced butter before the shoots were adequately broken down, resulting in weak extraction and loss of fat. Switching to avocado oil for the blend produced much better color and flavor. Consolidating the pea and pea shoot extraction into a single step simplifies the process and removes the butter risk entirely.

**Adjustments:**
- Replaced frozen peas (200g) with sugar snap peas (250g) — sweeter, more vibrant raw flavor
- Replaced separate cold infusion + pea puree steps with a single blanch (snap peas 45s, pea shoots 15s added at end) → blend with avocado oil → tamis
- Overnight rest is now of the full combined base (dairy + strained pea blend), replacing the vacuum-bag infusion step
- Added avocado oil (80g) as the fat carrier for the pea blend
- Reduced heavy cream from 350g to 200g to offset avocado oil fat contribution
- Increased whole milk from 260g to 300g to maintain base volume

## Method

**1. Make the dairy base.** Mix the milk powder and sugar in a small bowl. Combine cream, milk, and glucose in a stainless saucepan over medium-high heat. Whisk occasionally until it reaches a full rolling boil. Add the milk powder mixture, reduce to a low simmer, and cook 2 minutes, whisking constantly. Pour into a shallow bowl set into an ice bath; stir until cooled to 50°F or below. Transfer to the refrigerator and chill thoroughly — at least 4 hours.

**2. Blanch the peas and shoots.** Bring a pot of water to a boil and prepare a large ice bath. Drop the sugar snap peas in and blanch for 75 seconds, then add the pea shoots and blanch 15 seconds more. Transfer everything to the ice bath immediately. Drain well and gently squeeze excess water from the pea shoots.

**3. Blend and strain.** Combine the blanched snap peas, pea shoots, and avocado oil in the Vitamix. Blend on high for 90 seconds until completely smooth. Pass through the 60-mesh tamis, pressing firmly with a silicone spatula to recover all smooth puree and leave fibrous skins behind. You should have approximately 300–320g of vivid green blend.

**4. Combine and rest overnight.** Add the strained pea blend to the chilled dairy base and blend in the Vitamix on high for 30 seconds until fully combined and uniformly vivid green. Transfer to a vacuum bag, seal under full vacuum, and refrigerate 12–24 hours. *(No vacuum sealer: airtight container or zip-top bag with all air pressed out.)*

**5. Add xanthan.** Transfer the rested base to the Vitamix. With the motor running on low, add the xanthan gum and blend 15 seconds until fully incorporated.

**6. Churn.** Pour the base into the Whynter and churn until thick and soft-serve in consistency, 20–30 minutes.

**7. Harden.** Transfer to an airtight container, press plastic wrap directly onto the surface, cover, and freeze 4–12 hours before serving.

## Notes

- Sugar snap peas are blanched whole — no need to pod them.
- The staggered blanch (75s peas first, then 15s shoots) in one pot hits the right texture for each without a second setup. The longer blanch on the snap peas is intentional — it breaks down the waxy cuticle on the pod exterior, which is the primary source of waxy flavor and would otherwise carry through into the oil extraction.
- Avocado oil is neutral enough at this quantity that it carries fat-soluble color and flavor compounds without adding its own character.
- Never heat the combined base — add the strained pea blend only to a fully chilled dairy base to preserve the volatile top notes.
- If the green fades before churning (oxidation), a small amount of citric acid solution added after step 4 can help stabilize color without affecting flavor.
- Pea starch in the final base is ~0.4% — well below the 1.5% enzyme-treatment threshold. No treatment needed.
