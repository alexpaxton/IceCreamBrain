# Black (Like My Heart) Sesame

**Style:** Custard
**Makes:** between 1 and 1½ quarts

Black sesame seeds ground to a smooth paste anchor this custard in deep, roasted nuttiness — earthier and more complex than white sesame, with a natural bitterness that plays against the sweetness. Toasted sesame oil reinforces the character rather than adding separate notes. Food coloring is optional; the seeds alone push the base to a deep charcoal.

## Method

**1. Toast and grind the sesame.** Toast sesame seeds in a dry pan over medium heat, stirring frequently, until fragrant. Let cool completely. Grind in a spice grinder, processing in short cycles until a completely smooth paste forms, 10–15 minutes.

**2. Cook the custard.** Combine the sesame paste, whole milk, heavy cream, egg yolks, sugar, corn syrup, toasted sesame oil, and salt in a saucepan over medium heat. Blend occasionally with an immersion blender to prevent egg curds and keep the paste fully integrated. Heat to 82°C (180°F).

**3. Chill.** Transfer to a stainless steel bowl nested over a larger bowl of ice. Stir until cool, then cover and rest overnight in the refrigerator.

**4. Add xanthan and churn.** Blend xanthan gum into the fully chilled base until fully dispersed. Add black food coloring a drop at a time until the desired depth is reached. Churn per standard procedure.

## Notes

- 2g xanthan (double the standard 1g) reflects the high fat and solid load from sesame paste; reduce to 1g if the texture sets too gummy.
- Toasted sesame oil shares key compounds with the seeds (sesamol, 4-vinylguaiacol, pyrazines) but at higher concentrations; run ingredient-extraction on sesame oil for citable VOC data if needed.
